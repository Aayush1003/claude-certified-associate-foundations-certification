# 6. Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use

## Make the task safe, then do it

Responsible use is not limited to saying yes or no. First reduce avoidable risk, then perform the useful portion of the task within policy.

## Data and privacy checklist

Before sharing information, determine:

- What data classes are present?
- Is the tool approved for that data?
- Does the task actually need identifiers?
- Can names, emails, account numbers, and other direct identifiers be removed or replaced?
- Could free-text details identify a person indirectly?
- Who can access the Project, conversation, connector, and output?
- What retention, access, or contractual requirements apply?

An instruction such as “do not remember this” or “ignore this column” is not a substitute for a technical or organizational control. Once sensitive data has crossed a boundary, deleting the conversation does not undo the disclosure.

## Anonymization and pseudonymization

Remove or replace identifying information before upload when it is not needed. Review free text for unique events, locations, dates, job titles, and combinations of details that may re-identify someone. Keep the mapping separate and protected when reattachment is necessary.

Anonymization reduces risk; it does not automatically make every use safe.

## Fairness and responsible decisions

Check whether the input reflects historical bias, whether the task affects people, and whether the output could create unequal treatment. Do not delegate accountable decisions merely because the model can produce a recommendation. Define review, appeal, and escalation paths where appropriate.

## Policy and escalation

Follow organizational policy for approved tools, data, retention, access, and review. Escalate uncertainty instead of guessing, especially for legal, financial, medical, employment, safety, or regulatory decisions.

A disclaimer does not make inaccurate or unauthorized content acceptable.

## Risk-based review

Increase safeguards as impact increases:

- Low-impact internal draft: basic review.
- External communication: verify claims and accountable approval.
- High-impact or regulated decision: authoritative evidence, qualified human judgment, and policy-compliant controls.

## Self-check

1. Why is purpose alone not permission to upload personal data?
2. What is still risky about anonymized free text?
3. Does a system or Project instruction enforce a privacy rule by itself?
4. What should happen when a task is legally or financially consequential?

**Answers:** 1. Data policy and approved boundaries still apply. 2. Unique details can re-identify people. 3. No; instructions shape behavior but do not replace external controls. 4. Use authoritative sources, follow policy, and obtain qualified human review.
