# 8. Course Summary and Next Steps

## The complete operating loop

Use this sequence for almost any Claude-supported task:

1. **Frame:** define the business outcome, audience, risk, and success measure.
2. **Choose:** select the entry point, capability, model, and context.
3. **Prepare:** remove unnecessary sensitive data and curate authoritative sources.
4. **Prompt:** state the task, constraints, output format, and handling of unknowns.
5. **Execute:** decompose complex work and iterate on observable failures.
6. **Evaluate:** verify consequential claims, check completeness and bias, and adapt to the audience.
7. **Review:** keep human accountability where consequences, ambiguity, or reversibility require it.
8. **Improve:** measure quality, cost, latency, adoption, and failure patterns.
9. **Maintain:** update Project instructions, knowledge, access, and regression examples.

## Decision rules to memorize

- Evidence beats confidence.
- A gap is better than an invented answer.
- Source verification beats self-review.
- The smallest sufficient model is usually the right default.
- Durable context belongs in a maintained Project.
- Sensitive data should be minimized before upload.
- Human approval belongs at consequential boundaries.
- Diagnose the change before rewriting everything.
- Delegate bounded, checkable work; keep accountable judgment with people.
- Escalate integrations and technical implementation.

## Final revision routine

### First pass: recall
Write the seven exam domains and their weights from memory. Explain Projects, model tiers, validation, anonymization, workflow mapping, and escalation without notes.

### Second pass: application
For each practice question you miss, write the hidden constraint: accuracy, completeness, cost, speed, privacy, accountability, or maintainability.

### Third pass: timed work
Use the mock exams closed book. Answer every item, flag uncertainty, and return later. Review the rationale, not just the letter.

### Final readiness check

You are ready for focused revision when you can:

- Choose a model based on task requirements rather than prestige.
- Turn a vague business request into a realistic division of labor.
- Find omissions and unsupported claims in polished output.
- Explain why a Project needs maintained instructions and sources.
- Make a privacy-sensitive task compliant without abandoning it.
- Diagnose a regression using the exact changed conditions.
- State where human review and technical escalation belong.
